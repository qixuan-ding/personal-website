The goalpost keeps moving away.

I miss the days of being greeted by triple t, six sevens, and stream clips whenever I opened social media. Now, my feed is flooded with tech interview advice, self-improvement gym bros, and introspective views of relationships.

The internet, and especially social media, has made information more accessible than ever. To be clear, this is generally beneficial to me and broader society from a knowledge standpoint. But it's also taken a toll on me: **the sheer amount of internet exposure, especially online advice, makes me anxious due to constant comparison between me and an ideal version of myself.** I'm not sure where this causation stems from or how I can fix it, but I'll do my best to diagnose myself.

**People generally present themselves at their best on social media.** This makes sense. Especially for advice-oriented content, a creator needs to provide credibility or a hook for viewers to be engaged with their content: podcasts with new grads working in big tech, travel compilations of self-employed influencers, and jacked guys explaining the right exercise forms. These are obvious examples, and I believe this idea runs deeper. A lot of the content that I watch is about hardship in someone's life. And the way these reels, TikToks, or video essays are framed is about how they found *the light at the end of the tunnel*. These stories bring about hope: they reframe struggle as either something good happening to you or as fuel for personal success. But the feeling that online content like this presents to me is *increasingly manifesting as anxiety about my own life*.

If other people can achieve their crazy goals in life, despite their flaws, and despite their setbacks, then why can't I? Being exposed to all sorts of success stories puts a lot of pressure on me to not only pursue my dreams, but to succeed at them. It's overwhelming, because there seem to be a *million* different ways to learn, network, reflect, train, become a better person, and take risks. Especially at a pivotal point, having turned an adult, and with the actions I take in my teens to twenties apparently *deciding the entire trajectory of the rest of my life*. I'm at a stage where:
- **The world is my oyster**,
- Maximize experiences and variance,
- Minimize my future potential for regret,
- Grind toward my goals,
- But also be well-travelled and socialize,
- And find time for healthy living,
- Focus on depth in my area of expertise,
- But also develop a wide breadth of knowledge.
Every time I learn a new piece of information, an item gets added to the bottom of my infinitely long checklist of things to do, and a *few inches of necessary accomplishments get added to the turf between myself and the goalpost*. Even the struggle stories from above, designed to inspire hope, fill me with pressure to reach the expectation of eventual fulfillment and inner peace.

So how do I release the steam from my perpetual, internal pressure cooker? Here's what I'm going to try in my life:
1. No AirPods and scrolling during commutes and downtime. Especially at the gym.
2. Turn thoughts into actions. Keep myself busy with concrete work.
3. Break down goals. Recognize and order my long-term priorities and break them down into short-term goals. Then, divide the expectation by 2.
4. Relax properly. Watch a show or movie instead of a podcast or video essay.

I'm wondering if this is a more universal problem, or specific to my own overachiever mind stuck in social media's algorithmic loop.
